By the time I graduated high school, I had stood on a stage with a mic entertaining a crowd of people around 400 times. The biggest crowds were around 600, the smallest crowds around 30. The most intimate thing I had in my life was being on stage for the longest time. It's incredibly intimate, holding this device that can amplify your voice, and having an audience that waits. You bare yourself to them, and you bare yourself to yourself (at least if you have a good sound engineer). My favorite thing was to perform with another musician. Watch the eyes of the musicians singing together. They dance with each other exchanging glances, catching smiles, picking up dropped beats, and making mistakes that give their art humanity. The song ends, you drink your water. You look at each other, careful not to say anything so the mic doesn't pick anything up, and everything is said in a glance. You smirk, they smirk, and the next song begins.

I spend a lot of time thinking about how I think about art. I love art, and I love loving art. As do I love talking to new people. I love talking to new people and I love loving talking to new people. These are both things I monitor my own thought patterns in to increase my efficacy. ("If I approach this conversation with an open mind, it'll be more rewarding for the both of us... If I keep an eye out for things that resonate with my inner creativity, I have a larger vocabulary to be able to express myself...") I wonder if who we are lies in the differences of what people choose to metacognize about.

Metacognition is a skill in my emotion life that I am trying to get better at. What I choose to think about affects how happy I am. I can choose to think in a way that makes me happier, kinder, and more present. I can also choose to think in a way that excuses shortcuts and prefers short-term thinking. In this blog, I am going to write down about thoughts I think about.

## Grief brings out our full spectrum of humanity.

Most people do not like poetry. It's considered a waste of time. Until it's time to write a eulogy or your wedding vows. 

What is it that loss can bring out in us, and why is it so beautiful? I have held memories in my heart, that I know I won't be able to recreate, and felt only gratidude. I choose to continue to hold that gratitude. Grief and gratitude, I suppose, go part and parcel with one another.

## Choosing to be a winner.

When I was younger, I considered myself a historical inevitability. Life humbled me plus I wanted to fit in, so somewhere along the way I gave up my confidence. It wasn't a bad trade: I gained humility and some friends who were fun to pass time with. But, I'm realizing now that I would rather think of myself as a historical inevitability than not. The future is coming either way. My name will one day be spoken for the last time either way. Why not live my life grandiosely?

Than I asked myself, do I *even* live my life grandiosely? No—I live it one win at a time. And I keep losing, until I get the win. Whether it's something I'm learning or a benchmark I've set for myself in the gym. Most things in life are a matter of when, rather than if, if you can handle setbacks well. You don't need to be Caesar; you can just do the next thing on your to-do list.

## My favorite color.

I don't think too often about my favorite color. It's green. Seeing green makes me happier. Having more green in my life is worthwhile. Yet, I've never actually followed this line of reasoning. Color was always an expediency. *What color phone do you want?* Any will do.

Now as far as I know, I don't think Apple makes green MacBooks or phones. But, choosing to invest in making the space you occupy better is an exponential investment. Whether that means your own body's nervous system or your dorm room's decor. I think part of the reason why I resisted the label of STEM for so long is that aesthetic expression wasn't disliked, but neglected—something I had considered was worse. It's one thing to dislike a work of art that's abstract, it's another thing to wholly dislike abstract art.

## Forgiveness is a commitment, not a choice.

I don't think I have had to forgive many people in my life, and that's a good thing. I was thinking about why it's hard to forgive someone. And I think part of the disconnect is the person who did the wrong, doesn't understand it's not a choice. Forgiveness is a committment you make every additional day with that person. Some days I forget I've made that commitment and those days I carry deadweight in my heart.
